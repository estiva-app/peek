# AppShell

The frame of every page: the top bar, the side navigation, and the list, main
and replies columns, with Cmd/Ctrl+K opening the command launcher.

<Canvas of={AppShellStories.Default} />

## When

- Around every page in Peek. There is one frame and this is it.
- With `threadPanel` when a thread is open, which is what makes the third
  column appear.

<Canvas of={AppShellStories.WithThreadPanel} />

## When not

- Inside a story of a single part. A component's story should show that
  component, not the app around it.
- As a layout to copy. The three-column arrangement is the frame's; a page that
  wants columns of its own wants a grid.

## How

```tsx
import { AppShell } from '@/layouts/AppShell'

<AppShell leftPanel={<TopicList />} rightPanel={<Conversation />} threadPanel={thread} />
```

## What is the package's and what is Peek's

**The package's** (UIG-14 §9.3): the floating shell itself — the bar over the
top edge, the rail on the ground, the rounded card, and the Signal dot grid on
the ground, which the package's preset draws. The list column is the package's
`ListColumn` (UIG-14 N9), with its own error boundary.

**Peek's**: the bar's slots, the rail's entries, the three columns inside the
card, the launcher, and the collapsing. The frame only tells the list column
when the rail is closed.

## What it owns

- **The launcher's context, captured once.** It is read at the invoking
  keystroke or click — selection, then composer, then nothing — and **frozen for
  the launcher's lifetime**, so what the launcher acts on cannot change under it
  while somebody is typing into it.
- **Cmd/Ctrl+K**, in one place rather than per page.
- **The rail collapsing**, and telling the list column about it.
